\section*{Abstract [DRAFT]}

The quality of an SPP fit is typically assessed by distance between inferred and true generating process parameters. Existing methods - such as minimum contrast - require prior knowledge of the underlying family, and their performance degrades near complete spatial randomness (CSR): despite the generating process not being Poisson, its second-order structure (e.g. $K$-function) is almost indistinguishable from Poisson's. This is an inherent property of the problem, not a failure of methodology.

Instead, we evaluate a fit by generating simulations from the predicted parameters and scoring their fidelity to an independent held-out replicate, using a strictly proper scoring rule on local configurations, normalized between a Poisson (CSR) model (0) and the true model (1).

We propose a two-stage pipeline: a classifier determines the family, followed by family-specific parameter estimators. The first stage acts as a filter, routing near-CSR to the Poisson estimator.

Near CSR, patterns become inherently difficult to classify: a diverse set of classifiers (gradient-boosted trees, linear models, neural networks) over two feature types (summary functions, topological features) converge to similar accuracy, which degrades as a family's parameters approach the CSR limit.

Reproduction fidelity is $0.92$, despite the classifier only achieving $53\%$ accuracy.  

The inclusion of topological features never hurts parameter estimation, and helps substantially where second-order statistics are blind by construction: for the cell process they cut estimation error by $15\%$ and raise fidelity from $0.92$ to $1.00$.

\section*{The Story [INFORMAL]}

\begin{itemize}
    \item All models struggle with classification/inference on clouds near CSR. Unless you cherry-pick your prior (bad), you will have to deal with a large number of near-CSR clouds. Bad performance on these near-CSR clouds is not a failure of the models, it's an inherent property of the problem. 

    \item The pitch: we show that, despite this difficulty, classifiers successfully tell us which generating process is "good enough" for inference.

    \item Classification accuracy on the 8 processes seems week ($0.53$), but the final fidelity score - i.e. how well the inferred parameters reproduce the pattern - is high ($0.93$).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Task and criterion.} Single-pattern model fitting cast as predictive fidelity. A strictly
    proper kernel score on radius-$R$ local configurations is anchored at the true model (regret) and at CSR
    (gain), giving skill $=1-\sum\text{regret}/\sum\text{gain}$. Fits are scored on an \emph{independent}
    replicate, so a fit cannot be flattered by the pattern it was estimated from. A power check shows the obvious
    alternative (a Wasserstein-1 energy score) fails.
  \item \textbf{The CSR wall.} Across 8 families and 11 classifiers / 10 estimators, detection, classification
    and parameter error collapse at the same boundary, described by one physical coordinate per family (cluster
    overlap $\omega$, signal-to-noise $\zeta$) times a power of $\nb$. The proper score confirms the boundary
    independently of any learned model: there, even the true model does not beat CSR.
  \item \textbf{A pipeline that is the posterior's factorisation.} $p(f,\theta\mid x)=p(f\mid x)\,p(\theta\mid x,f)$:
    one classifier (its $p(\text{poisson}\mid x)$ \emph{is} the CSR filter), then per-family estimators. The flat
    classifier beats a hierarchical cascade (group, then family, with a reject class) by $5.4$ points.
  \item \textbf{Where topology helps.} Adding PH to classical summaries never makes estimates significantly
    worse and helps most where second-order statistics cannot see the structure (cell process: $K(r)=\pi r^2$
    exactly; ring process: loops in $H_1$).
  \item \textbf{Negative results, reported.} Wasserstein scoring has no power; training-set filtering by regime
    helps in regime but cannot be exploited when the regime must be inferred.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Presenting the weak numbers}

None of these can be fixed by more modelling (the ceiling row above); all can be presented honestly and to our
advantage. Put them in the main text next to their explanation, never only in the appendix.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Accuracy $0.53$.} Report it against the regime coordinate, not only pooled: the pooled value depends
    on how much prior mass sits near CSR, which is our design choice ($0.65$ in the strong-signal regime). Add an
    equivalence-aware accuracy that merges families which coincide in part of their parameter space, justified by the
    fidelity of misassigned patterns (skill $0.95$).
  \item \textbf{Thomas recall $0.17$.} Thomas is the limit of three other families (nested with vanishing
    meta-clusters, ring with large jitter, and LGCP at second order). Report the parsimony operating point as the
    demonstration that recall only moves between families.
  \item \textbf{Mat\'ern II recall $0.34$} ($57\%$ called Mat\'ern I). At low packing both are hard cores of the same
    radius; Mat\'ern II fidelity is $1.00$.
  \item \textbf{Thomas estimator error $0.70$.} Report the identifiable combinations ($\nb$, $\omega$, $\sigma$) beside
    $\kappa$ and $\mu$; the $\kappa$--$\mu$ ridge motivates posteriors as future work. In regime: $0.45$.
  \item \textbf{Wrong-family skill $0.74$ over all strata.} Skill is unstable where the true model barely beats CSR;
    quote it only where the gain is resolved ($0.95$).
  \item \textbf{PH end to end.} Fidelity is saturated; PH buys parameter accuracy where second-order statistics are
    blind. Cell is the one candidate for an end-to-end effect \pending{cell-only run}.
\end{itemize}

  \item Fig.~1 and the per-model regime curves.
  \item Confirm the AISTATS 2027 deadline; historically it falls in early October.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Cheap and worth it.} Soft routing (simulate from the family mixture weighted by $p(f\mid x)$, using existing
posteriors); a calibration check of $p(f\mid x)$.

\textbf{Future work, framed as the upgrade the factorisation allows.} Posterior rather than point estimates for $\theta$
(distributional trees or neural posterior estimation) and posterior-predictive sampling; learned permutation-invariant
summaries of the raw points (DeepSets / GNN) as the stronger learned baseline.

\textbf{Anticipated reviewer questions.} Why not a classical fit? (\S5 baseline.) Why only synthetic data? (real-data demo.)
Why point estimates? (limitations; soft routing partly answers it.) Is PH just another feature? (\S6: it helps exactly where
second-order summaries are blind, and never hurts.) Why is accuracy so low? (\S4: the CSR wall; lead with it, do not hide it.)
