\section{Diagrams}
Compute and vectorize $H_0$ and $H_1$ persistence diagrams of each cloud. We consider multiple filtations: Rips, Alpha, and DTM$_k$.
The latter is swept over $k \in \{5, 10, 15, 20\}$.

%==============================================================================
\section{Persistence images}
We follow (Adams et al.\ 2017), with some small adjustments.

\subsection*{Procedure}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rescale.} Multiply all coordinates by $\sqrt n$: lengths are then in units of the mean point spacing $1/\sqrt n$, and the image is divided by $n$. Without this a pattern at $\nb=800$ occupies a third of the axis of one at $\nb=100$ and carries $\approx7\times$ the mass, so resolution and dynamic range would both track $\nb$
  \item \textbf{Coordinate transformation.} $(\text{birth},\text{death}) \to (\text{birth},\text{persistence})$
  \item \textbf{Smooth.} Each pair becomes a Gaussian of width $\sigma$ centred on it, weighted linearly by its persistence (diagonal points are weightless)
  \item \textbf{Rasterise.} Pixel values are the integral of the surface over its box
  \item \textbf{2-D or 1-D.} $H_1$ is always 2-D. $H_0$ is 2-D only under DTM; Rips and alpha have degenerate $H_0$ births ($=0$), so their $H_0$ image is the persistence axis alone
  \item \textbf{Standardise.} Images pass through $\sqrt{\cdot}$, then a $z$-score with one (mean, sd) per filtration channel.
  A linearly weighted $H_1$ image is $\approx93\%$ empty with a few very bright pixels.
  $\sqrt{\cdot}$ is variance-stabilising.
  \item Resolution: $64 \times 64$
  \item $\sigma = 1$ pixel
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Calibration}
The axes of the persistence diagrams are calibrated. Diagrams across the same homoogy dimension 
need to share the same axes in order for the vectorization to be meaningful.
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fitted on training clouds only, once per (filtration, dimension), then frozen and applied to validation and test. The $z$-score is fitted the same way
  \item Each training diagram contributes its own min birth, max birth and max persistence, so clipping is a property of a whole pattern rather than of its busiest corner
  \item The box holds the full support of \textbf{coverage} $=99\%$ of training diagrams: 
  \[
    \text{persistence} \in [0,\ 1.05\cdot q_{99}(\text{max persistence})],
    \qquad
    \text{birth} \in [q_{1}(\text{min birth}),\ 1.05\cdot q_{99}(\text{max birth})],
  \]
  the factor $1.05$ a pad so the $99\%$ diagram's own tail is not on the edge
\end{itemize}

\section{Summary functions}
The classical arm featurizes the same clouds without a filtration.

\subsection*{Procedure}
Let $\hat\phi$ be any estimator taking a cloud $x$ to a real function of a single scale,
$r\mapsto\hat\phi_x(r)$ on $(0,r_{\max}]$. Featurizing it is sampling it at $512$ radii.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Sample.} Fix a grid $0<r_1<\dots<r_K=r_{\max}$ shared by every cloud, and take
  \[
    x \;\longmapsto\; \big(\hat\phi_x(r_1),\ \dots,\ \hat\phi_x(r_{512})\big)\in\R^{512}
  \]
  \item \textbf{Standardise.} A $z$-score per function: one (mean, sd) pooled over training clouds \emph{and} radii, so the curve is shifted and rescaled, never reshaped per radius or per cloud
  \item No need for $\sqrt{\cdot}$: bounded functions, not a sparse measure with a heavy tail
\end{itemize}

\smallskip
Nothing here is specific to the four summary functions below.
Any estimator indexed by a scale (e.g. pair correlation function, Betti curve, $\hat K$ under a different edge correction) vectorizes the same way.
The only choices are $r_{\max}$ and where the $K$ samples sit.

\subsection*{Functions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $L(r)-r = \sqrt{\hat K_x(r)/\pi}-r$ (isotropic edge correction), $=0$ under CSR
  \item $F(r)$: empty-space function, the distance from a fixed location to the nearest point. Sees voids
  \item $G(r)$: nearest-neighbour function, the distance from a typical point to its nearest other point. Sees short-range clumping or inhibition that $\hat K$ averages over
  \item $J(r) = (1-G)/(1-F)$: $=1$ under CSR, $<1$ clustering, $>1$ inhibition
\end{itemize}
Estimation details:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $F$ and $G$ use the reduced-sample (border) correction: keep only the points, or test locations, further than $r$ from the edge of $W$,
  so nothing is censored by the window. Exactly unbiased, no tuning constant
  \item $F$'s test locations are a $64\times64$ interior grid, $\approx15\times$ oversampled against the sweep's median $n=282$
  \item $J$ is held flat past the last radius with $1-F>0.05$ and clipped at $5$: an unbounded ratio entering a $z$-scored channel would swamp it
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Grid}
Two conventions, because the four functions do not vary on the same axis.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{fixed}: $r\in(0,0.25]$ in 512 equal steps
  \item \texttt{sqrtn}: $r = u/\sqrt n$, $u\in(0,2]$ (same $\sqrt n$ rescaling the images apply to diagram coordinates)
\end{itemize}

\smallskip
$F$ and $G$ are distance CDFs and reach $1$ near the mean nearest-neighbour distance $\approx 1/(2\sqrt n)$.
On \texttt{fixed} they sit at exactly $1$ over $62\%$ and $66\%$ of their samples, against $23\%$ and $31\%$ on \texttt{sqrtn}, and the saturation point moves by $4.7\times$ across the sweep's range of $n$ ($44$--$961$): one encoder then sees the same rise at very different positions.
