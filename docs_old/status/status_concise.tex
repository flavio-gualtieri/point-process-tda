\section{Summary}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Classical summary functions beat persistence images on every parameter-estimation family and on classification overall (accuracy \cBestAcc\ vs.\ \cPhAcc).
  \item \textbf{The one place PH beats a classical model:} classification in the two most structured bands, against $L$ alone (the published baseline): \cVsLModerate\ and \cVsLStrong\ points of accuracy. It is behind the best classical model in every band.
  \item On parameter estimation PH is behind $L$ alone in every band on every family.
  \item The PH deficit is concentrated near Poisson, where the classical 5\% test itself cannot detect a departure: \cVsLBelow\ points against $L$ alone.
  \item $H_0$ carries most of the persistence information; $H_1$ adds little, and on two families slightly hurts.
  \item \textbf{The PH numbers are a lower bound.} Every PH run so far uses one filtration scale (DTM, $k=5$), and for classification omits the point count $n(x)$ that every classical model receives.
\end{itemize}

\section{Data and methods}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Models:} Poisson, Thomas, nested Thomas, Mat\'ern~II (hard-core), LGCP on the unit square, with expected count $\nbar \in [100, 800]$.
  \item \textbf{Distance from Poisson, $\dtil$:} the largest standardised deviation of the model's theoretical $L$-function from Poisson, divided by the 5\% critical value of the classical global envelope test. So $\dtil \le 1$ means exactly that the classical test does not reject Poisson.
  \item \textbf{Training:} 10{,}000 patterns per model from the prior (fixed 85/15 train/validation split).
  \item \textbf{Test set:} 50{,}000 patterns, 2{,}500 in every cell of 5 models $\times$ 4 bands: \texttt{below} ($\dtil<1$), \texttt{weak} (1--2), \texttt{moderate} (2--4), \texttt{strong} ($\ge 4$). Poisson patterns are split evenly across the bands so chance is 0.20 in each. Selected from patterns that already existed; no model was retrained. Details: \texttt{docs/test\_set.md}.
  \item \textbf{Classical:} 1-D CNN of Vihrs (2022) on summary functions plus $n(x)$. All 15 subsets of $\{L,F,G,J\}$ were run.
  \item \textbf{PH:} CNN on persistence images of the DTM filtration ($k=5$): $H_0$, $H_1$, or both as two channels; plus $n(x)$ for parameter estimation only.
  \item \textbf{Training:} early stopping on validation loss; mean $\pm$ SD over 10 seeds.
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The best classical subset differs by family ($L{+}F{+}G$ on Thomas and nested, $G$ on Mat\'ern~II, $L{+}F{+}G{+}J$ on LGCP), but is within about one seed SD of $L$ alone except on nested (0.229 vs.\ 0.239).
  \item PH ($H_0{+}H_1$) has \pRatioLo$\times$ (\pRatioLoFam) to \pRatioHi$\times$ (\pRatioHiFam) the loss of the best classical model.
  \item The gap (Table~\ref{tab:params-gap}) is smallest near Poisson (1.02--1.18) except on Mat\'ern~II (2.17), peaks at \texttt{moderate} on Thomas and nested and at \texttt{weak} on Mat\'ern~II, and is flat at $\approx$1.1 on LGCP.
  \item Against $L$ alone the ratios are similar, and above 1 in every cell: PH never beats the published baseline on parameter estimation.
  \item Mat\'ern~II is the largest gap (up to 4$\times$). DTM with $k=5$ averages over five neighbours and so smooths away the minimum interpoint distance that defines a hard core.
  \item $H_1$ alone is weak. Adding it to $H_0$ helps on Thomas (0.231 vs.\ 0.249) and LGCP, and slightly hurts on nested (0.302 vs.\ 0.287) and Mat\'ern~II.
\end{itemize}

\section{Classification}

5-way classification (Poisson, Thomas, nested, Mat\'ern~II, LGCP), all models trained on the same pooled training set. Metric: accuracy (chance 0.20 in every band).

\begin{itemize}
  \item \cBestName\ is the best classifier (\cBestAcc), with $L{+}J$ and $L{+}F{+}J$ at 0.717. $L$ alone: \cLAcc.
  \item PH $H_0{+}H_1$ reaches \cPhAcc: \cGapBest\ points behind the best model and \cGapL\ behind $L$ alone. The deficit is on Mat\'ern~II (recall \cRecMaternPh\ vs.\ \cRecMaternBest) and Thomas (\cRecThomasPh\ vs.\ \cRecThomasBest). PH has the best Poisson recall of any model shown.
  \item \textbf{By band, against $L$ alone:} \cVsLBelow\ / \cVsLWeak\ / \cVsLModerate\ / \cVsLStrong\ points. PH overtakes the published baseline once the structure is comfortably detectable. Against the best classical model it stays behind in every band, by 2.1 points at \texttt{strong}.
  \item Near Poisson PH is conservative: it labels 52\% of the structured \texttt{below} patterns as Poisson, against 35\% for \cBestName. That is both why its Poisson recall is highest and why its \texttt{below} accuracy is lowest.
  \item As a Poisson test every model holds the 5\% false-alarm rate. PH has about 9--13 points less power in \texttt{below} and \texttt{weak}; from \texttt{moderate} every model except $H_1$ detects essentially everything.
  \item $H_1$ alone is above chance (\cPhOneAcc) but the weakest classifier and detector. Adding it to $H_0$ is worth half a point (\cPhAcc\ vs.\ \cPhZeroAcc).
\end{itemize}
